# The hedge engine

## The problem in one line

`1 Delta contract ≠ 1 Hyperliquid coin`, and the ratio between them is
different for every pair.

From the shipped catalogue (generated by `hedgelab instruments sync` from the
venues' own metadata), computed by the engine:

```
1 BTCUSD contract  (1 contract = 0.001 BTC)  = 0.001 BTC  on Hyperliquid (1 unit = 1 BTC)
1 ETHUSD contract  (1 contract = 0.01 ETH)   = 0.01 ETH
1 PAXGUSD contract (1 contract = 0.001 PAXG) = 0.001 PAXG
1 SOLUSD contract  (1 contract = 1 SOL)      = 1 SOL
```

Note BTC and PAXG share a ratio of `0.001` while one contract is worth ~$82
against ~$4. Equal ratios do not mean equal economics, which is why the engine
converts through base units on every call rather than caching a ratio.

The domain still models lots, CFDs and inverse contracts generically — the
unit tests exercise all three — but every shipped instrument is a linear
perpetual: Delta in contracts, Hyperliquid in coins on a `10^-szDecimals`
lattice (BTC 0.00001, ETH 0.0001, SOL 0.01, PAXG 0.001) with a $10 minimum
order value.

## The conversion

Everything routes through one projection so heterogeneous instruments become
comparable:

```
position (qty in venue units)
   → base_units        = qty × units_per_quantity          (linear)
                       = qty × contract_size / price       (inverse)
   → notional_quote    = base_units × price                (linear)
                       = qty × contract_size               (inverse, price-independent)
   → quote_delta       = base_units                        (linear)
                       = notional_quote / entry_price      (inverse)
   → account_delta     = quote_delta × FX(quote → account currency)
```

The FX step is not decorative on this pair: Delta India settles in USD and
Hyperliquid in USDC, and the USDC/USD rate is measured from Kraken's public
ticker (0.99985 in the committed recording) rather than assumed to be 1.

### The inverse-contract trap

Neither venue currently lists a live inverse perpetual, so no shipped pair uses
this — but the engine models it and the unit tests pin it down. An inverse
perpetual is denominated in *quote* units. Long `N` quote units entered at `E`,
marked at `S`:

```
PnL_base  = N × (1/E − 1/S)
PnL_quote = PnL_base × S = N × (S/E − 1)
d PnL_quote / d S = N / E          ← depends on the ENTRY price, not the mark
```

while the mark-to-market base holding is `N / S`. So for a $500,000 inverse
position entered at 100,000 and marked at 125,000:

| Objective | Hedge |
|---|---|
| `BASE_ASSET_NEUTRAL` | 4 BTC — matches the 4 BTC you currently hold |
| `QUOTE_PNL_NEUTRAL` | 5 BTC — matches the actual price sensitivity |

A 25% difference in hedge size, on the same position, depending on which
question you are asking. Both answers are correct; picking the wrong one leaves
a quarter of the book unhedged. Tested in
`test_inverse_base_neutral_and_delta_neutral_diverge_when_in_profit`.

## The nine objectives

| Objective | Neutralises | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| `BASE_ASSET_NEUTRAL` | base-asset units | you care how much BTC you hold |
| `NOTIONAL_NEUTRAL` | notional in the account currency | the legs track different underlyings |
| `QUOTE_PNL_NEUTRAL` | `dPnL/dS` in quote terms | you care about price sensitivity |
| `ACCOUNT_CCY_PNL_NEUTRAL` | `dPnL/dS` after FX | the legs settle in different currencies |
| `CUSTOM_RATIO` | delta × an explicit ratio | you have a view |
| `PARTIAL` | delta × a ratio below 1 | you want deliberate residual exposure |
| `FUNDING_ADJUSTED` | mean-variance optimum vs carry | carry is expensive |
| `COST_ADJUSTED` | mean-variance optimum vs carry + execution | carry *and* fees are expensive |
| `RISK_WEIGHTED` | beta-adjusted delta | the legs are imperfectly correlated |

They are not interchangeable. On 5000 BTCUSD contracts (5 BTC) hedged on
Hyperliquid, from the committed recording
(`hedgelab calculate --source PAPER_DELTA:BTCUSD --hedge PAPER_HYPERLIQUID:BTC --quantity 5000 --objective …`):

```
BASE_ASSET_NEUTRAL       -5        BTC   residual  1.50 bps
NOTIONAL_NEUTRAL         -4.99877  BTC   residual  3.96 bps
QUOTE_PNL_NEUTRAL        -5        BTC   residual  1.50 bps
ACCOUNT_CCY_PNL_NEUTRAL  -5.00075  BTC   residual  0.0002 bps
```

The 1.5 bps floor on the "exact" objectives is the USDC/USD rate of 0.99985: a
hedge that is perfectly delta-neutral in quote terms is *not* neutral in
account terms. `ACCOUNT_CCY_PNL_NEUTRAL` drives that to zero, and on
Hyperliquid's 0.00001 BTC lattice the rounding residual that is left is
negligible. `NOTIONAL_NEUTRAL` sizes on dollar value, so the gap between the
venues' prices (Hyperliquid's mid 81,685.5 against Delta's 81,653.25 at that
frame) moves it off five coins. The engine's job is to show you which residual
you are choosing.

### The mean-variance objectives

`FUNDING_ADJUSTED` and `COST_ADJUSTED` minimise carry against the variance of
the hedged position:

```
Var(h) = σs² − 2·h·ρ·σs·σh + h²·σh²

min_h  h·k + (λ/2)·Var(h)     ⟹     h* = β − k / (λ·σh²)      β = ρ·σs/σh
```

The optimum is the minimum-variance ratio **β**, pulled down by a carry term
scaled by the **hedge leg's** variance. This also makes the objectives
consistent with one another: `RISK_WEIGHTED` is exactly the `k = 0` case.

> **A bug worth recording.** An earlier version divided by the *residual*
> variance, `σs²(1−ρ²)`. Against the shipped default parameters that produced
> 0.92 and looked entirely plausible. Once volatility was estimated rather than
> assumed, correlation measured far closer to 1 than the assumed 0.995 —
> making the residual variance roughly 300× smaller and the carry penalty 300×
> too large. The objective returned **zero** for a pair whose hedge
> demonstrably removed almost all of the drawdown. The error was invisible
> against assumptions and obvious against measurements, which is the argument
> for estimating in one sentence. (Found on the price simulator that predates
> the switch to real data; the regression test still guards it.)

**`k` is the marginal cost, not the total.** The source position's funding is
paid whether or not it is hedged, so charging it against the hedge would argue
for not hedging a position precisely because its own funding is expensive —
which is backwards. Only the hedge leg's funding enters `k`. Enforced by
`test_adaptive_objectives_ignore_the_source_leg_funding`.

**`k` can be negative.** Hyperliquid's BTC funding sat at its 0.00125 %/h
baseline for most of the committed month — 3 bps a day, paid *to* a short. A
short hedge leg is then paid to exist, and the objective leans the ratio up,
not down.

Calibration: with a hedge-leg vol near 3%/day, `λ = 250` means a hedge costing
1 bp/day trims the ratio about 0.4 percentage points below β. At the measured
BTC vol of ~1.79%/day the same carry trims about 1.25 points, because the
penalty scales with `1/σh²`.

Behaviour under assumed and measured statistics (measured = the estimator over
the committed three days of 5-minute candles, 863 samples):

| | β | no carry | HL baseline, short receives (−3 bp/day) | paying 3 bp/day | paying 30 bp/day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| assumed (σ=3%, ρ=0.995) | 0.995000 | 0.995000 | 0.996333 | 0.993667 | 0.981667 |
| measured (ρ=0.9953) | 0.985252 | 0.985252 | 0.989015 | 0.981489 | 0.947623 |

The trim is proportional to carry, symmetric in its sign, and scaled by the
hedge leg's variance — which is the property the earlier formula lacked.

### Where the statistics come from

`marketdata/stats.py` estimates σ and ρ from real prices. At startup it is
seeded with real 5-minute candles — from `HEDGELAB_MARKET_HISTORY_PATH` if set,
otherwise fetched from both venues in live mode — and then keeps sampling the
feed the rest of the platform sees. Three decisions, each a real trade-off:

* **Statistics are computed in float, not `Decimal`.** Everywhere else this
  platform uses `Decimal`, because a quantity that misses a venue's lattice by
  one ULP is a rejected order. A volatility estimate is not that kind of
  number: its sampling error after a few hundred observations is several
  percent, around fourteen orders of magnitude larger than float precision.
  The boundary is explicit — floats live inside that module, results leave as
  `Decimal`.

* **Returns are normalised by *observed* elapsed time**, not by the configured
  sampling interval. The interval is a minimum spacing, not a guarantee: when
  the feed ticks more slowly, every observation covers more time than
  configured, and scaling by the configured value overstates volatility by
  `sqrt(actual/configured)` — a silent 3.46× error when a five-minute setting
  meets an hourly feed. Dividing each return by `sqrt(elapsed)` removes the
  dependence entirely, and handles an irregular feed for free. Tested by
  feeding real hourly candles to an estimator configured for five minutes.

* **Sampling is coarse on purpose.** On the committed live recording, sampled
  every 2 seconds, Delta↔Hyperliquid BTC correlation measures **0.64** and β
  0.65; on 5-minute candles over three days it is **0.9953**. At short
  horizons the two venues' books update independently and the basis (it
  averaged $26 across the recording, between $12.75 and $38.25) dominates the
  return. Since β = ρ·σs/σh, sampling every tick would corrupt every
  risk-weighted hedge. The default is one sample per five minutes.

Below `min_samples` (30) the estimator refuses to answer and the objectives
fall back to the configured assumptions, with a warning on the calculation
saying so. A number derived from eight observations should not be presented as
a measurement.

There is no configured "true" volatility to recover any more, so accuracy is
checked the other way round: the estimator's answer equals the realised
volatility computed directly from the same closes to within 1e-8. Daily
volatility on the committed 5-minute history:

```
instrument                  daily vol

PAPER_HYPERLIQUID:BTC         0.01786
PAPER_DELTA:ETHUSD            0.02518
PAPER_HYPERLIQUID:ETH         0.02531
```

### Beta comes with its uncertainty

β is the OLS slope of source returns on hedge returns, so it has the usual
standard error:

```
SE(β) = σ_residual / (σ_hedge · √n) = σ_source · √(1−ρ²) / (σ_hedge · √n)
```

Reporting the point estimate alone invites reading sampling error as signal —
and for a hedge ratio, "reading it" means trading it. Delta BTCUSD on
Hyperliquid BTC, feeding the committed 5-minute history in order:

| samples | β | s.e. | 2 s.e. band | distinguishable from 1? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 1.0105 | 0.0122 | [0.9862, 1.0349] | no |
| 100 | 0.9931 | 0.0105 | [0.9721, 1.0141] | no |
| 216 | 0.9885 | 0.0071 | [0.9743, 1.0027] | no |
| 432 | 0.9890 | 0.0047 | [0.9797, 0.9983] | **yes** |
| 863 | 0.9853 | 0.0033 | [0.9787, 0.9918] | **yes** |

Hyperliquid's BTC 5-minute returns run about 1.5% larger than Delta's. On the
first eighteen hours (216 samples) that is inside the noise and the dashboard
labels it "not distinct from 1"; on three days it is measurable, and the
engine says so.

The estimator keeps a rolling window of 500 samples, so a running instance
reports the most recent 500 rather than all 863 — on the committed history plus
the recording, `RISK_WEIGHTED` uses ρ = 0.9945 and β = 0.9783.

Correlation is not uniform across pairs:

| pair | ρ | β | distinguishable from 1? |
|---|---|---|---|
| BTC Delta ↔ Hyperliquid | 0.9953 | 0.985 ± 0.0033 | yes |
| ETH Delta ↔ Hyperliquid | 0.9948 | 0.990 ± 0.0034 | yes |
| SOL Delta ↔ Hyperliquid | 0.9886 | 0.997 ± 0.0052 | no |
| PAXG Delta ↔ Hyperliquid | 0.9433 | 0.974 ± 0.0117 | yes |
| BTC Delta ↔ PAXG Hyperliquid | 0.274 | — | — |

Tokenised gold's daily volatility is roughly 1%, against ~1.8% for BTC and
~2.8% for SOL, while the cross-venue basis noise is not proportionally smaller
— so the matched PAXG pair correlates noticeably worse. That is the correct
answer, not a defect, and it is exactly the kind of thing an assumed constant
hides.

## The optimiser

The exact solution rarely lands on the venue's lattice. Rounding is one choice;
searching the neighbouring points is another. Hyperliquid's lattice is fine, so
the stakes are smaller than on a lot-based broker, but the choice is still
real. 3737 PAXGUSD contracts (3.737 PAXG), `NOTIONAL_NEUTRAL`, on the 0.001
PAXG lattice, from the committed recording
(`hedgelab optimize --source PAPER_DELTA:PAXGUSD --hedge PAPER_HYPERLIQUID:PAXG --quantity 3737 --objective NOTIONAL_NEUTRAL`):

```
  qty      residual    cost   margin    score
 -3.741     9.20 bps   9.97    1526    0.5111
 -3.740     6.52 bps   9.97    1526    0.3872
 -3.739     3.85 bps   9.96    1525    0.2632   ← naive rounding of the exact -3.739026
 -3.738     1.18 bps   9.96    1525    0.1977   ← STEP_SEARCH / MIN_RESIDUAL
 -3.737     1.50 bps   9.96    1525    0.1767   ← WEIGHTED default
 -3.736     4.17 bps   9.96    1524    0.2363
```

The residual column measures account-currency delta, while the objective
neutralised notional — so the exact notional answer is not the delta minimum.
Rounding gives −3.739 (3.85 bps). `MIN_RESIDUAL` finds −3.738 (1.18 bps). The
weighted default prefers −3.737, the base-neutral point, because it also weighs
cost, funding and margin; that is a *choice*, and the candidate table makes it
visible rather than implicit.

Three modes: `EXACT` (round), `STEP_SEARCH` (best on one named priority),
`WEIGHTED` (normalised weighted sum over seven priorities). Scoring normalises
each metric against the observed range in the candidate set, so weights are
comparable across basis points and dollars, and a metric that is identical
across candidates contributes nothing instead of dominating by scale.

## Every calculation shows its work

`HedgeCalculation.steps` is an ordered list of `(label, formula, value)`. The
dashboard renders it, the audit log stores it in the `CALCULATED` transition
payload, and the CLI prints it. A hedge number nobody can reconstruct is not
usable in production.

From `hedgelab calculate --source PAPER_DELTA:BTCUSD --hedge PAPER_HYPERLIQUID:BTC --quantity 5000`
on the committed recording:

```
 1. Market data         BTCUSD mid / BTC mid                81653.25 USD / 81685.5 USDC
 2. Contract sizing     1 contract = 0.001 BTC | 1 base_unit = 1 BTC
 3. FX conversion       USD/USD = 1, USDC/USD = 0.99985
 4. Source exposure     5000 × 0.001 BTC                    base=5 BTC, notional=408266.25 USD, delta=5
 5. Hedge exp. per unit 1 base_unit of BTC                  base=1 BTC, notional=81673.25 USD, delta=1
 6. Objective           qty = −source.quote_delta × ratio / hedge_unit.quote_delta   −5 base_units
 7. Venue rounding      step=0.00001, min=0.00001, toward zero   −5 → −5 (error 0)
 8. Residual exposure   source + hedge (signed)             base=0, delta=0, ratio=1
 9. Execution cost      fees 183.7648 + half-spread 2.4996 + slippage 0   186.2644 USD
10. Carry (per day)     source funding −1.0742 bps/day; hedge funding −3.0000 bps/day   166.3663 USD/day
11. Margin              |notional| × initial_margin_rate 0.025   10209.16 USD
12. Expected P&L        carry 166.3663/day × 1d − execution 186.2644   −19.8981 USD
13. Worst case (99%)    expected − z₉₉·|residual|·σ·√days   −22.1966 USD
14. Break-even move     execution cost / source notional    0.045623 %
```

The residual in step 8 is zero in quote terms and 1.50 bps in account terms —
the USDC/USD rate again — and the calculation carries a warning saying so.
In that frame Delta's BTC funding was negative (−0.0036 % per 8 h, paid to the
long) and Hyperliquid's sat at its +0.00125 %/h baseline (paid to the short),
so on this snapshot both legs *receive* carry. That is one frame; over the
committed month Delta's averaged +0.0035 % per 8 h and the long leg paid — the
30-day backtest in the README shows how it nets out.

## Rounding policy

Quantities round **toward zero** by default. Under-hedging leaves a known
residual the rebalancer can close; over-hedging creates exposure in the
opposite direction that nobody asked for. A quantity below the venue minimum —
including Hyperliquid's $10 minimum order value — becomes zero and is flagged,
never silently promoted up to the minimum, which would execute more than the
calculation asked for.
